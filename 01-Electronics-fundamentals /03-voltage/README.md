Voltage

Overview

Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points. It describes the energy transferred per unit charge.

Key Concepts

- Voltage is measured in volts (V).
- One volt equals one joule per coulomb.
- Formula: (V = W/Q)
- A 9 V potential difference corresponds to 9 joules of energy transferred per coulomb.
- A battery can maintain voltage across its terminals without an external circuit.
- Sustained current through an external circuit requires a complete conducting path.
- Voltage polarity indicates which point is at a higher electric potential.
- Reversing voltmeter probes reverses the sign of the reading.

Voltage, Current, and Resistance

Quantity| Meaning| Unit
Voltage| Energy transferred per unit charge| Volt (V)
Current| Rate of electric charge flow| Ampere (A)
Resistance| Opposition to current flow| Ohm (Ω)

Practical Understanding

A battery can measure 9 V while a bulb remains off if the circuit is open. The battery maintains a potential difference, but there is no sustained current through the bulb.

Learning Outcome

- Explain voltage as energy per unit charge.
- Distinguish voltage from current.
- Explain why voltage can exist without external current.
- Interpret voltage polarity and voltmeter readings.
