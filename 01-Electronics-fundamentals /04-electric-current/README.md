Electric Current

Overview

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge passes through a point in a circuit.

Formula

[
I = \frac{Q}{t}
]

Where:

- (I) = current, measured in amperes (A)
- (Q) = charge, measured in coulombs (C)
- (t) = time, measured in seconds (s)

One ampere means one coulomb of charge passes a point per second.

Key Concepts

- In metal wires, electrons are the mobile charge carriers.
- Electron flow in the external circuit is from negative to positive.
- Conventional current flows from positive to negative in the external circuit.
- Voltage provides an electric potential difference that can drive current.
- Resistance opposes current flow.
- In a series circuit under steady-state conditions, the same current flows through every component.
- Current is not consumed by components; charge is conserved.
- Electrical energy can be converted into light, heat, motion, and other forms.
- An open circuit prevents sustained current through the external circuit.

Example Calculation

If 10 coulombs of charge pass through a wire in 2 seconds:

[
I = \frac{10}{2} = 5\text{ A}
]

The current is 5 amperes.

Learning Outcome

- Calculate current from charge and time.
- Distinguish electron flow from conventional current.
- Explain the relationship between voltage, resistance, and current.
- Explain why current stops when a circuit is opened.
- Distinguish charge flow from energy conversion.
