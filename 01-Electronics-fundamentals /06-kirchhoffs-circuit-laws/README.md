Kirchhoff's Circuit Laws

Overview

Kirchhoff's circuit laws describe how current and voltage behave in electrical circuits. They are based on the conservation of electric charge and energy.

1. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)

The total current entering a junction equals the total current leaving it.

Formula:

Total current entering = Total current leaving

Example:

A total current of 30 mA enters a junction. One branch carries 12 mA.

Current in the other branch:

30 mA − 12 mA = 18 mA

2. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)

The algebraic sum of voltage rises and drops around a closed loop equals zero.

In a simple circuit, the battery's supply voltage equals the sum of the voltage drops across the components.

Example:

A 12 V battery supplies two series resistors. The first resistor drops 5 V.

Voltage across the second resistor:

12 V − 5 V = 7 V

3. Applying Kirchhoff's Laws with Ohm's Law

Ohm's law:

V = I × R

Example:

A 12 V battery supplies two series resistors. The first resistor drops 4 V, and the circuit current is 2 A.

Voltage across the second resistor:

12 V − 4 V = 8 V

Resistance of the second resistor:

R = V / I = 8 / 2 = 4 Ω

Key Takeaways

- Current is conserved at circuit junctions.
- Voltage rises and drops balance around a closed loop.
- Parallel branches share the same voltage when connected across the same two nodes.
- Ohm's law can be combined with Kirchhoff's laws to calculate unknown circuit quantities.
