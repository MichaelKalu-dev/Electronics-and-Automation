Electric Charge

Overview

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter responsible for electrical interactions. It can be positive or negative and is measured in coulombs (C).

1. Subatomic Particles

Particle| Charge
Proton| Positive (+e)
Electron| Negative (−e)
Neutron| Neutral (0)

The magnitude of the elementary charge is approximately:

[
e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}
]

2. Net Electric Charge

An object's net charge depends on the balance between its positive and negative charges.

- Equal numbers of protons and electrons produce zero net charge.
- An excess of electrons produces a negative net charge.
- A deficit of electrons produces a positive net charge.

Example:

An object with 10 protons and 13 electrons has a net charge of:

[
Q = 10e - 13e = -3e
]

[
Q = -4.806 X 10^{-19}C
]

3. Methods of Charging

- Friction: Electron transfer between materials brought into contact and rubbed together.
- Conduction: Charge transfer through direct contact with a charged object.
- Induction: Charge redistribution caused by a nearby charged object without direct contact. Grounding can allow a net charge to remain.

In ordinary electrostatic charging, electrons transfer between objects while protons remain bound in atomic nuclei.

4. Conservation of Electric Charge

Electric charge is conserved. In an isolated system, the total charge remains constant, although charge can move between objects.

Example: When a balloon gains electrons from hair, the balloon becomes negatively charged and the hair becomes positively charged.

5. Conductors and Insulators

Conductors: Materials in which electric charge can move relatively freely. Examples include copper and aluminium.

Insulators: Materials in which charge cannot move freely through the material under ordinary conditions. Examples include plastic and rubber.

Conductors provide paths for current, while insulation helps prevent unintended electrical contact.

Key Takeaways

- Electric charge can be positive or negative.
- Electrons carry negative charge; protons carry positive charge.
- An object's net charge depends on its charge balance.
- Charging commonly involves electron transfer or charge redistribution.
- Charge is conserved.
- Conductivity depends on how readily charge carriers move through a material.
